# Alternate forms in serif typefaces (2026-11-10)

Which letters professional serifs offer alternate forms for, and how many designs exist. Sources: the feature tables of
16 OFL serifs from Google Fonts' repository and the serifs installed on this machine (read with fontTools), plus
foundry feature pages (Commercial Type, Klim, Grilli Type, Dinamo, Fontshare's API) read by a research agent. Shapes
named only by a foundry label are marked unverified.

## Finding

Text serifs carry few letter alternates. Tiempos, Lyon, Georgia Pro, Domaine Text, Newsreader, Crimson Pro and
Libre Baskerville have no letter stylistic sets; their options are figures (old-style, tabular), small caps and case
punctuation. Letter alternates live in display and editorial faces (Serrif, ABC Arizona, GT Super, Canela, Fraunces,
Instrument Serif, Publico Headline). Faces meant for reading keep one unambiguous default and put choice in figures.

## Legibility

No foundry statement says one-story versus two-story a matters on screen; research on misread letters (Beier and
Larson 2010) favours two-story a. Apple's accessibility guidance: type I, l and 1 and check they differ. The usual fixes
are a serifed I, a footed l, a flagged 1 with a foot and a slashed or dotted 0, which Area Serif's defaults already do.
