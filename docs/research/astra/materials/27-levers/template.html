Material levers

Materials taken apart into separate settings, the way Layers splits tone into ground and contrast. Each lever is one number from 0 (off) to 8, with the parts it applies to. Glass, Tactile and Soft become saved combinations of lever values, and you can make any other.

How the levers work

  • Grouped as Figma groups them. Fill (gradient, sheen, transparency, frost), Stroke (style and strength) and Effects (inner shadow, emboss, contact shadow, glow), plus Page (grain, page glow). Figma stacks fills, strokes and effects on a layer the same way.
  • One light for everything. The light angle (0 = top, clockwise) sets the gradient's direction, the radial highlight's position, the sheen, the lit edge of a stroke, which side an inner shadow falls on and where the contact shadow lands.
  • Each lever says where it applies, and each part group has a strength. Groups: Primary (solid buttons, the selected segment, thumbs, ticked boxes), Secondary (outline and soft buttons), Badges (solid badges), Fields (fields, tracks, unticked boxes), Selected rows (menus, nav), Cards and Overlays (menus, popovers, toasts). Ghost buttons, text, tables, lists and code take nothing. By default Primary takes the material at full strength and the rest echo it at half, as platforms, libraries and the old system styles do (29-scope).
  • Every lever works on the Layers tone. A gradient lightens and darkens the part's own tone, transparency lowers its alpha, strokes and shadows use white and black at an alpha. Colour comes only from the accent, by choice (D5).
  • Nothing else splits this far. Figma has the same parts but per layer. daisyUI 5 has system-wide --depth and --noise, but each is on or off and bundles several paints. Radix and shadcn make panels or menus translucent as one switch. None gives each paint its own scale, targets and a shared light.
  • Updated from your eight references. A ring takes the part's own colour, darker (Tone; Neutral is black, as on the board's dark button). The bright inner edge away from the light is its own lever, Catch-light. Wells flip their gradient. A radial gradient can centre toward the light, in the middle, or away from it. How see-through a part can go depends on what it holds.
  • Safe by construction. Transparency stops at the floor for what a part holds; reduced transparency and forced colours turn transparency and frost off; levers never change size, type or ink (D16).

Questions for you

  • One value or one per theme? Layers has separate light and dark settings. The levers here are one number each, tuned per theme inside (dark strokes and shadows are stronger). Separate light and dark values would double the panel.
  • Where do targets live? Per lever (as here), or one target set for the whole material (simpler, less control)?
  • Contact shadow: material or Elevation? It could be an Elevation role for controls instead of a material lever.
  • Elevation for cards. Reference 1's card shadow is far softer and wider than Elevation's resting role can draw. Widen that role, or add a softness setting to Elevation?
  • Gradient fill colour. Tone-on-tone and accent-tinted are here. More colour (a second hue, mesh) would need a colour source beyond accent and tones.
  • Text on gradients. A strong gradient on a card darkens one end. At 8 in light, muted text at the dark end of a card falls under 4.5:1 unless the gradient is capped there or Layers solves inks for it.
