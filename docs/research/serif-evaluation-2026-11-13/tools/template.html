    Proportions

    Units of 1,000 per em unless a ratio. Contrast is the lowercase stem over the thinnest part of o. Advance is the mean lowercase advance.

    Outline over outline

    Area Serif filled, the other face as a red line, scaled so the x-heights (or cap heights) match and aligned on the left edge of the ink.

    Texture

    The same paragraph at 15 px in every face, x-heights matched, so the difference you see is weight, width and spacing.

    Tests

    Five groups, each run on Area Serif and on every reference with the same code, so a number is read against what good typefaces actually do. A finding is a place where Area Serif disagrees with itself or sits outside what the references do.

    1 · Curves

    Curvature turning points, 62 core characters

    Each outline is cut at its corners, curvature is sampled every 2 units, and each rise-and-fall is counted. Fewer is smoother; a bracketed serif face has two per bracket by design.

    Node tests on Area Serif's cubic source

    Curvature combs, every core character · select one to inspect it

    2 · Thicks and thins

    Capital thin strokes, as a share of each face's own O hairline

    One dot per stroke: A and H bars, E F L T Z arms, E and F middle arms, B middle, P and R closing strokes. A tight cluster is one hairline.

    Lowercase thin strokes, as a share of each face's own o hairline

    Bars of e f t z, the tops of c e a s, the arches of n h m, the base of u, and the bowls of b d p q where they leave the stem.

    Round strokes at their thickest, as a share of the stem

    Lowercase o c b d p q against the n stem; capitals O C G Q D P R and B's lower bowl against the H stem.

    Stems

    Every upright stem in the face, by scanline. Spread is the largest minus the smallest.

    Stroke thickness along the outline thin thick

    Each dot is the distance across the stroke at that point, measured along the inward normal where the far edge is parallel. Colour should change evenly from thick to thin.

    3 · Serifs

    Feet on i l h n m and capitals H I K F P, in units. A range of one value means every serif of that kind is identical. Flag slope is the angle of the head serif's top edge.

    4 · Alignment and spacing

    Area Serif against its own lines
    Spacing rhythm, all 3,844 pairs of the core 62

    Optical gap: the mean distance between two letters over their shared height, with kerning. Variation is its standard deviation over its mean; lower is more even. f pairs are left out of the narrowest gap because the test runs without ligatures.

    Area Serif's 24 narrowest pairs, live

      Method

      How it was tested

      • Curves: node tests on the cubic source of all 1,001 glyphs (tangent breaks, curvature jumps at smooth nodes, inflections and double turns inside a curve, handle reach, missing extremes, near-upright handles and lines), plus a curvature profile sampled every 2 units on every face.
      • Thicks and thins: scanlines every 10 units for stems and every 5 for level strokes; a ray across the stroke at every outline sample for round strokes; 130 named parts measured per face.
      • Serifs: projection, tip thickness, bracket curve at three points, bracket height and cup for every foot and head on an upright stem; projection, height, tip and slope for every flag; ball sizes from the parts audit.
      • Alignment and spacing: every flat edge and extreme against the face's lines; all 3,844 pairs of the core characters shaped with kerning.
      • Then every one of the 62 core characters was drawn large with its points, handles and curvature comb and looked at, and each flagged place was zoomed.

      Not checked
