Your three references, read from the pixels

Sampled across each edge at 2× (device pixels), with no source CSS. They share one recipe: a hairline stroke near 10% black, a short shadow that darkens the bottom edge more than the top, and on surfaces a 1px white line just inside the top.

What the research found

Twenty sources, read from source or live CSS (Radix, Geist, Primer, Tailwind, shadcn, Polaris, Atlassian, Material 3, Fluent web and WinUI, Linear, Stripe, Raycast, Resend, Clerk, Ant, Apple, the CSS specs, and the shadow generators). The full record with every layer value is docs/research/elevation-stroke-2026-10-18/sources.md.

  • A shadow is a list of slots, not one value. Radix, Geist, Primer and Tailwind all compose a fixed order: inner highlight or shade, ring, lip, then one to five soft layers. Primer keeps its ring slot at 0% when off so a theme can switch it on without changing the layer count.
  • Geometry, colour and alpha are separate. Tailwind rewrites every layer's colour through one variable and its alpha through another. Josh Comeau and Stripe tint the shadow toward the background's hue.
  • Every generator shares one model. One final layer (distance, blur, alpha) is spread over N layers with an ease-in (smoothshadows: t = ((i+1)/n)²; Brumm: a bezier), spread grows negative with distance, and a light angle sets x from y. Material 3 and Fluent are two-layer cases of it.
  • Resting shadows are small and common. shadcn puts 0 1px 2px 5% on every field and outline button and shadow-sm on cards. Primer's buttons carry a 0 1px 0 4% lip. Radix's segmented thumb and classic card share a five-layer resting stack.
  • Hover grows the shadow, never the stroke width. Material steps one level, Fluent shadow4 → shadow8, Radix grows offsets and blurs at about the same alpha. No system surveyed moves the element on hover.
  • Contrast never strengthens a shadow. Systems darken strokes (Primer, Polaris, Material's outline targets), turn translucent rings opaque, or replace the shadow with a 50% inset ring (Atlassian). Forced colours remove every shadow. Nobody thickens 1px to 2px for contrast; thicker lines are kept for focus and selection.
  • Stroke widths. Apple's hairline is one device pixel; Polaris draws 0.66px; Radix adds a 0.5px ring. Scales stop at 1 / 2 (Primer, Fluent, Polaris, Clerk). Light strokes are translucent black (Apple 10%, Geist 8%) or opaque grey (shadcn, Polaris); dark strokes are nearly always translucent white, 7–17%.

Where Area is today

  • Elevation is one number: the alpha of a fixed two-layer Fluent shadow per role (control, resting, tooltip, float, modal). Distance, blur, spread, layers, colour and light don't move.
  • Surface is the frame width, 1 or 0. At 0 a soft 0 0 1px 62% edge stands in.
  • At rest only cards, panels, Composer, thumbs and floating chrome carry a shadow. Fields and buttons have none.
  • Contrast moves every stroke to the 3:1 stroke (since today) and touches no shadow. At Surface 0 with More, a surface draws both its frame and the soft edge, a double edge.
  • Material already draws a ring, lit edge and double rim (its Stroke lever), and contact and emboss shadows. Its rules replace the whole box-shadow, so under any material Drawer and Command palette lose their modal lift and a selected Card loses its ring.

Proposal

Keep every current option. Turn Surface and Elevation into two groups of levers in the Inspector, each a number with named ends like the settings beside them, and give every role the same fixed list of slots so steps and hover crossfade layer by layer.

How it meets Contrast

  • Medium and More keep moving every stroke toward the 3:1 stroke from wherever Stroke strength puts it. Shadows stay as they are, as in every system surveyed.
  • Hairline becomes 1px at Medium and More; nothing goes thicker than 1.
  • The soft shadow edge switches off whenever a raised level brings the frame back, ending the double edge.
  • The lip and highlight are decoration and stay; under forced colours every shadow goes, so each floating surface keeps a real (transparent) border to fall back on, as today.

How it meets Material

  • One owner per effect. Edge and Depth would own every outer shadow (soft layers, lip, contact) and the frame. Material keeps fills and inner light (gradient, sheen, transparency, frost, inner shadow, catch-light, emboss, glow, grain).
  • Material's contact lever and its ring and lit-edge stroke styles would become the Lip, Highlight and Stroke levers; a bundle would set them, as bundles already set the Layers. No option is lost.
  • Components would list Material's layers beside the role instead of replacing the whole box-shadow, which also fixes the modal lift, selected Card ring and Video player ring that materials drop today.
