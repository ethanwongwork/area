3. Series colours from OpenAI's palette

Eight hues sampled from your references: sky, pink, yellow-green, lilac, peach orange, mint, periwinkle, peach, in the validator's best order. On white the luminous colour fills bars and areas and a deeper point on the same hue (3:1 on the page) draws lines, dots and the fill's edge, as GitHub Primer pairs its data colours. On the dark page the luminous colour is the mark itself (10:1 or more). The sliders move the luminous colours on every hue at once.

Validator: the dataviz skill's checks (OKLCH lightness band, chroma ≥ 0.10, Machado 2009 colour-vision ΔE between neighbours, a normal-vision floor of 15, 3:1 against the page). The luminous colours sit above the skill's lightness band on purpose (brighter, as you asked); on white they are about 2:1, so a luminous fill needs its edge or labels. At one lightness, four hues can't all stay apart for colour-blind readers when any two touch (all-pairs, first four); stacked and side-by-side charts only put neighbours together.

5. Fills that vanish at Layers 0

Table's header paints bg-subtle, which at Layers 0 (the default) is the page itself. Inline code paints bg-stripe, 1.03:1 on white. Proposed: a fill role that always steps from its ground (bg-component: #f9f9f9 light, #161616 dark), for the components that mean "a fill" rather than "a recessed layer".
