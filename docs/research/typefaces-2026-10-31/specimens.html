Area typefaces

Open-licensed faces for font switching, display faces to pair with a plain body, and a check of the type ramp. Every specimen is set on Area's ramp at type 13: 11/14 small, 13/18 UI, 15/22 reading (15/24 as prose), 22/30 title-md, 30/38 title-lg, 52 display. Strong and bold weights are each face's measured match for Geist's 500 and 600.

Text faces

For UI and body. Each has variable weight, a true italic (Google Sans Flex slants), and x-height at least 0.70 of its cap height. With the x-height match on, every face renders at Geist's 0.53 x-height, which is how the switch would keep the ramp, icon pairing and row heights. Turn it off to see the faces at their own sizes.

Display faces

Only for title-md and up and the display sizes. The body stays the text face: Geist under each, in grey. "Same as text" stays the default.

Like ABC Areal

ABC Areal is Dinamo's, and its licence forbids public repositories and servers, so it can't ship in Area. What sets it apart is a neo-grotesk with flat-cut terminals and Mono and Semi Mono siblings that share its shapes. These are the open faces built the same way: a grotesk with its own mono, or one file that moves between them.

Display from independent foundries

Libre faces from foundries designers know: Velvetyne, Collletttivo, MCKL, Kanon, Monokrom, Commercial Type, Bonjour Monde, Tyler Finck. Each covers Latin Extended (Polish, Czech, Turkish), so it can carry a product's titles. All are SIL OFL. Body under each is Geist.

English only

Respected and libre, but drawn for a short character set: a product title in Polish, Czech or Turkish falls back to another face mid-word. Fine for a fixed English headline or a logotype, not as a system's display face.

Licensed faces

From Klim, Displaay, Pangram Pangram, Dinamo, Schick Toikka and Fontshare. Each needs a licence, and none can ship in Area or be offered as a built-in choice. Fontshare's FFL 2.0 says so in so many words (§02, no "selectable font for third-party users" in a design tool). What Area can do is what Figma does: use a face installed on your Mac, measure it there to set its weights and size, and mark a system that names it as needing a web licence. These are images rendered from the trials on your Mac and Fontshare's downloads, at type 13; trial files are cut to basic Latin.

Worth a trial

Not on your Mac yet. Each foundry offers free trial files for evaluation.

Code faces

For Code, Kbd and code blocks, at text-sm.

The ramp

Eight sizes and two weights, at type 13. Product UI has every role it needs: caption, UI text, reading text and three titles. The gaps are in running text and display, below.
