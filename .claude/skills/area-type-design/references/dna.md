# Typeface identity

Adapted from Nicolas Massi's `type-dna.md` (Apache-2.0; see NOTICE.md). Use it when the owner asks for a face to feel
more like something, or less like a reference.

A face's identity is a system of three layers. Changing one surface detail rarely changes the identity while the
construction underneath stays the same.

| Layer | Variables | Look at |
|---|---|---|
| Structural | Skeleton and construction; proportion and width system; stress | `H O n o a g R Q S 2 4 7 &`; `H/O`, `n/o`, `R/H` ratios; x-height, cap height, ascender, descender; `O o e c S` |
| Modulating | Weight and contrast; tension; counters and apertures; terminals and joins | Stems and hairlines; how curves meet straights; `c e a s` openings; ball, teardrop, beak, bracket |
| Textural | Spacing and rhythm; color; kerning behavior | Strings and paragraphs at size |

Tension is not one number: it comes from where handles sit, stress, counter pressure, aperture, and how fast a curve
turns into a straight.

## Changing a face on purpose

1. Settle `H O n o` (proportions, stems, rounds, counters, spacing).
2. Use `O o` for stress, contrast, counter pressure and tension.
3. Use `S a g Q R 2 4 7 &` for distinctive construction.
4. Check spacing, rhythm and color in text.
5. Refine terminals, joins and traps last.

Pick the lever from the diagnosis: too close to a reference in structure, change skeleton or proportion first; right
structure but wrong flavor, change contrast, tension, apertures and terminals; weak texture, fix spacing and color
before redrawing.

## Area Serif's identity today

- Structural: Libre Baskerville's transitional skeleton (vertical stress, ball terminals, swash Q, crossed W), with a
  screen face's proportions: x-height 520, cap height 712 (x/cap 0.73), ascenders 1.50 and descenders 0.43 of the
  x-height, J on the baseline.
- Modulating: a light text weight (stem 0.158 of the x-height), low contrast (1.8), serif tips 27 with softened
  corners, ball terminals 15% fuller than the source.
- Textural: Libre Baskerville's proportions, not narrowed (n advance 1.28 x-heights); its kerning, scaled.
