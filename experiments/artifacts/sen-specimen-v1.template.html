Sen, drawn from the centre of Noto Sans JP

A kanji and kana face made of one line. Each stroke is the centre line of Noto Sans JP's Thin weight, found by thinning, rebuilt as cubic Béziers, and drawn with flat ends at a single width. Where a stroke meets another it ends exactly on that stroke's centre line, so no character opens a gap at any weight.

No gaps, at any weight

語, the bottom left corner of its 口, enlarged. The thin red line is the centre line; the blue square is the bar's end, stopped on the upright's centre line.

A stem of width w ends on the centre line of a bar of the same width, meeting it at angle θ. Its flat end is a segment of length w centred on that line, so its two corners lie at most

d = (w/2) · |cos θ| ≤ w/2

from the bar's centre line: inside the bar's ink, which reaches w/2 either side. Nothing is left open and nothing pokes through the far edge. The rule holds for any w, so it holds across the whole weight axis. Corners inside one stroke, like 口's top right, are mitred.

Weight

One line, thickened. Steps are even to the eye: the line width grows by the same ratio at each step, from 16 to 110 units.

Width

The skeleton stretches across; the line stays the same width, so a narrow cut is not a squeezed one.
