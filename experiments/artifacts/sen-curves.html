Sen, one line through eight kanji

One line of one width, flat ends and round corners, drawn through the centre of Noto Sans JP. Points sit only where they have to: at ends, at corners, and where a curve can't be one cubic. A straight run is a single line; handles lie along the line they continue, or level or upright.

Skeletons. Centre lines of Noto Sans JP Thin (SIL OFL 1.1), found by thinning its outlines at 2 units a pixel. Units are thousandths of the em.

Points. Each stroke is smoothed (Gaussian, σ 10 units) and drawn with the fewest points that stay within 5 units of it: one curve first, then its straight runs as lines, then one more point at a time, placed where the stroke turns exactly level or upright when it does. Short hooks at free ends are cut.

Curves. Hobby's method, as in METAFONT: through each smooth point the direction is solved so that curvature runs on unbroken, and handle lengths follow from the angles. A single curve between two set directions takes its handle lengths from the stroke, never reaching past where the two directions meet. No curve dips past its ends without a point there.

Corners. Strokes meeting in an L become one path; each corner is a circular arc of radius 40 written as one cubic. Ends that meet another stroke stop on its centre line, so nothing opens at any weight.
