Sen, rounded and bent

Twelve square studies of one line drawn through the centre of Noto Sans JP. Each sets a Japanese word that names what it does: corners filleted with true arcs, strokes bowed, softened, slanted, swept and drawn in one line, and the same line as a tube, a shadow, dots or rails. After every change, joined ends are snapped back onto the stroke they meet, so nothing opens.

Skeletons. Centre lines of Noto Sans JP Thin (SIL OFL 1.1), found by thinning and fitted with cubic Béziers. Units are thousandths of the em.

Corners. A corner turning by φ with radius r is cut back t = r·tan(φ/2) on both sides and joined by one cubic with handles (4/3)·tan(φ/4)·r, the usual circular arc, within 0.03% of the circle for a right angle.

Words. 角, 曲, 丸文字, 斜体, 筆記体, 一筆書き, 袋文字, 影文字, 点線, 二重線 and 雲 are ordinary Japanese words. The last study sets the first sentence of the Japanese Wikipedia article 漢字 (CC BY-SA 4.0).
